# Interface design

[Back to the README](../README.md)

Thoric's appearance draws on the Asgard ships and interiors that inspired the project: dark neutral metal, with color kept for what it means. That influence belongs in the colors, the type and a few details. The interface itself follows the shadcn/ui `dashboard-01` block, so it reads like a familiar admin dashboard: readable previews, tables you can scan and controls you can find quickly.

## What the interface should communicate

A user should be able to see which containers are running, open one, and understand whether an operation succeeded. Make those actions and states easy to recognize before adding decoration.

Use familiar names: **Dashboard**, **Containers**, **Images**, **Templates**, **Volumes**, **MCP**, **Files**, **Jobs**, **Activity**, **Dependencies** and **Management**. Add explanatory text only when it helps someone make a decision or understand a result. A snapshot dialog should explain what is saved; a header does not need to explain that the app is a local workspace manager.

Write visible copy in English in `core/web/src/locales/en.ts`, and give the same key a translation in every other catalog there; see [Translate the interface](DEVELOPMENT.md#translate-the-interface). Write each language as natural interface text of its own rather than word for word, keep placeholders where the sentence needs them, and keep labels short enough for a button in every language. Errors should say what failed and, when possible, what the user can do next. Successful jobs should describe the outcome rather than display an unexplained ID; a job names the container or image it worked on.

## Layout

The layout is `dashboard-01`, filled with Thoric's own data:

- **Sidebar** (inset variant, 18 rem): the hammer mark and name with the release the manager runs, **New container** with a hammer button for **Build image**, eight entries, with **Endpoints**, **Desktop servers**, **Inspector** and **Activity** below **MCP**, a section that only names them and opens the first, and **Jobs** and its in-progress badge below **Management**, while that entry or one of its own pages is open, a **Running containers** group where the block lists documents, links to the documentation and the MCP guide, a small card naming a newer release with **Update** where the WSL package takes it, and the account menu with the theme choice and sign-out. While an update runs, the card follows it until the page reloads into the new manager. It collapses off screen with the header button or Ctrl+B, and is a sheet below 768 px.
- **Site header**: the sidebar button, the page's icon and title, after the entry it belongs to and a chevron for the pages under **MCP** and for **Jobs** and **Dependencies** (**Management**), the page's main action and, at the right edge of every page, **Jobs**. Its button carries the number of jobs in progress in a round `--primary` badge, and its menu lists those jobs first, then the latest, up to six, each with its state icon, name, target and stage or state, with **All jobs** last. The word hides below 640 px, leaving the icon and badge. A lost Docker connection shows as an alert on the pages that need Docker instead.
- **Dashboard**: four summary cards, resource usage and the job activity chart, in that order. The block has one chart; resource usage is the second, in the same card style. Containers are listed only on their own page.
- **Containers**: one card per container with its preview; **Images**: one table with the catalog images first; **Templates**: one card per template, with a search field and a select of the shared tags with their counts, applications first and environments in a section of their own below them; **Volumes** and **Jobs**: tables; **Endpoints**: a table, with **Add /mcp to a client** and **New endpoint** in the heading; **Desktop servers**: a card per container with its servers; **Inspector**: a card to choose and connect a server, then its items beside the chosen one's detail, one column on a narrow page, and the traffic below, its server kept open while another page under **MCP** shows; **Files**: a container picker in the heading and one card with the file explorer the volume browser uses too; **Management**: a card with this release, the newest one and its actions, and a footer with the license notice that links to **Dependencies**, then one card per section of the configuration, two columns on a wide page, with each setting's name in the mono face, what it does below it and its value beside it, or below it on a narrow card. A feature's `<NAME>_ENABLED` and its `<NAME>_DEFAULT` share a row, found by their names, each half with its value beside its name at every width and the halves stacked on a narrow card; a setting that the switch it needs leaves without effect, a default whose feature is off or the microphone's without sound, reads as off, cannot change and names that switch below what it does. A secret shows a lock and whether it is set, never a value. Where the WSL package takes changes, an editable setting has its control in place of its value, a switch, a number with its unit, a select of its meaningful values with a field for one of the user's own, or a text field, with a **Changed** badge in the `--edit` color and an undo button once changed and the reason below it when refused; the others carry a **CLI only** badge. While there are changes, a bar with their count, **Discard** and **Apply and restart** sticks to the bottom of the page. **Activity**: the filters in one row that wraps on a narrow page, then the calls grouped by day, one dense row each with a rail and an icon in its kind's color, the time, the caller, the tool, the container, the summary in the mono face, the outcome and the duration; a row opens a drawer with the call's details and its masked arguments. **Dependencies**: the release in the heading with **Download JSON**, then the interface, backend and infrastructure behind the same view switch as **Jobs**, a filter of kinds, scopes and licenses with their counts and a search, and one table each, names and versions in the mono face, the description below the name.
- **Desktop view**: full screen without the sidebar. A 48 px toolbar leaves as much room as possible for the remote desktop; **Jobs** stands before the fullscreen button, its word shown from 1280 px like the other tools. The desktop is scaled to fit and keeps its resolution; **Resize to window** is a pressed toggle, off unless the browser remembers it on, whose tooltip says at every width that agents see the new size and that the desktop goes back to the resolution of its settings once the toggle is off or the view disconnected; its word shows from 1536 px, so every tool fits at 1280. Below 640 px the tools keep only their icons in narrower buttons, so all of them fit beside the name.
- **Requests for a person**: an agent's request waits above the content of every dashboard page, below the site header, as a card with a warning edge and tint: the agent's container in the heading, how long ago it asked beside it, the message, then **Open desktop**, an optional reply and **Decline** and **Done**, which wrap below the reply on a narrow page, where **Open desktop** keeps only its icon. The desktop view shows that container's requests in a strip of the same tone under its toolbar, which scrolls after 40% of the height so the desktop keeps its room.

Pages live in the URL fragment (`#/images`, `#/desktop/<id>`) so they survive a reload and can be bookmarked.

The surfaces are the neutral palette of the shadcn preset, without a blue or warm cast. The primary color is near white on dark and near black on light, and it marks what the user can act on. Hue is kept for meaning: each kind of thing has its concept color, described below, and green, amber and red carry states. Always pair a status color with text and an icon: `StatusBadge` does this for every state. Menu items color their icon by what they do, through `tone` on `DropdownMenuItem`, so a long menu can be scanned: green starts, connects or issues a token, amber stops, retries or revokes, `--info` shows, `--transfer` moves files and `--edit` changes settings. Their text stays in the foreground color, except the destructive item, whose text and icon are red; keep the delete item last, after a separator. A chart whose series are outcomes uses those colors too: the job activity chart draws completed jobs in `--primary` and failed ones in `--destructive`, because two greys cannot be told apart on a dark card. The agent activity log marks a destructive call in `--destructive`, a change in `--edit` and a read in `--muted-foreground`, each with an icon and its word, and a failed call with a red badge apart from its kind. The usage charts tell containers apart, so each running container gets one of the `--series` hues in their order and keeps it while it is listed; past five they share the grey `--series-other`. That order was checked with a color vision deficiency validator against both card surfaces: every adjacent pair stays distinct. Three light-theme hues sit below 3:1 against the white card, so the legend names each one and a table under the charts gives the current figures; never let the hue alone say which container is which. The host GPU is not a container and is drawn in `--primary`. There is no background lighting; leave remote desktop screenshots in their original colors.

Each kind of thing Thoric manages has a concept color: containers, images, templates, volumes and MCP, which covers endpoints and desktop servers. Wherever a thing is named, in the sidebar and the page header, a row, a select item, a group heading, a drawer title or a badge saying what kind it is, its icon comes from `ConceptIcon` in `core/web/src/components/concept.tsx` in that color, so a list or a menu says what each item is before it is read. A badge naming a kind is `ConceptBadge`, and an empty state's icon sits on a tint of its color through `ConceptMedia`. An icon on a button that does something, such as **Connect**, stays as it is. The color is set inline, so a focused menu or select item, which recolors its children, keeps it. Jobs, files, activity, dependencies and management are views rather than things and stay neutral. The concepts share hue families with the menu tones, a container with `--info`, a volume with `--transfer` and a template with `--edit`, because five concepts and three states leave no room for more hues: a tone colors what an item does, a concept what a thing is. The snapshot item takes the image color, since it makes an image. To add a concept, add its name to `CONCEPTS` in `core/web/src/lib/concepts.ts` and its icon to `CONCEPT_ICONS`, which TypeScript refuses to leave out, give it a hex value in both themes of `index.css` with its `--color-concept-*` utility, and a row in the table above. `core/web/tests/concepts.test.ts` holds every concept color to 4.5:1 on cards, menus and the sidebar in both themes, and to an OKLab distance of at least 0.08 from the other concepts and the state colors; the menu tones get the same contrast check.

Everything that floats is glass: a translucent surface over a 40 px blur of what lies behind it (`backdrop-glass`, saturated by half), edged by `--glass-ring` and lifted by the deep `shadow-glass`. Menus, selects, suggestion lists and toasts use `--glass-raised`, one step lighter than a dialog in the dark theme, so a menu opened over a dialog never shares its tone. In the light theme both are white and the ring and shadow set them apart; a dialog stays nearly opaque there, because white over the dimmed overlay turns grey and washes out the input borders. Tooltips stay inverted. The dropdown menus lost a `::before` backdrop in Chromium, so a menu's blur sits on the menu element itself, and submenus are portaled so a blurred menu never clips what opens from it. When the system asks for less transparency or more contrast, every glass surface turns opaque and its ring firms up; a browser that cannot blur gets the opaque surfaces too.

## Type and spacing

The interface uses Oxanium for text and Geist Mono for code, both bundled from Fontsource packages so nothing loads from the network. Logs, image references, volume names and container IDs use the mono face. Build scripts, compose setup scripts, the build configuration, text files on the Files page and the MCP inspector's JSON are written in `CodeEditor`, which is Monaco, the editor of VS Code, loaded the first time one shows and themed with the `--code-*` colors. It colors bash and JSON, and a file in any other language Monaco knows by the file's name; configuration files Monaco has no language for color as INI, and shaders as C++. In a dialog a script is a card instead (`script-field.tsx`): its first lines colored and fading out, its length and **Edit**, so the form keeps one scrollbar, and selecting it opens the script in an editor over the dialog, 16 px in from the window's edges, which fills that space and scrolls by itself. The editor works on a copy: **Done** or Ctrl+S keeps it, **Cancel** and Escape drop it and ask first when something changed, and the parts of a merged build share one editor with a button for each. A pasted configuration and a file fill their view or pane. An inspector's JSON field grows with its text up to a set height and then scrolls. Other fields stay plain `Textarea`s. On a touch screen, which Monaco does not support, and until it has loaded, code falls back to `CodeTextarea`, which colors bash or JSON, does not wrap lines and numbers them in a gutter that scrolls with the field, and to its read-only `CodeView`. Sizes follow the shadcn components: 14 px body text, 12 px supporting text, 16 px page titles in the header and larger numbers only on summary cards.

The radius is 10 px (`--radius: 0.625rem`); the components derive smaller and larger corners from it. In the Maia style buttons, inputs and badges are fully rounded and cards use the 2xl corner. Pages use the block's spacing: 16 px side padding, 24 px from 1024 px, and 16 to 24 px between sections.

## Components

Components come from shadcn/ui (Radix base, Maia style) in `core/web/src/components/ui/` and are project code once added. Prefer an existing component to new markup, and new variants to one-off classes. Icons come from Remix Icon (`@remixicon/react`), the preset's icon library: use the `Line` icons, and a `Fill` icon only where the filled shape carries the meaning, as the success state does.

- **Summary cards** show a number, a badge and two lines of context. Use honest numbers from the API; there is no trend data, so do not invent trends.
- **Tables** use `core/web/src/components/data-table.tsx`: the block's view tabs (a select on narrow pages), sorting on columns where order matters, and pagination that stays on its page while the list refreshes. A list the manager could not read says so, with its reason and **Try again**, in place of the empty state, on the card pages as in the tables.
- **Detail drawers** open from a table row, from the right, or from the bottom on a phone, as the block's row viewer does. The container drawer holds the preview, the configuration and every action; the job drawer lists a job's stages, with the running one's bar under it, and the parts and steps a build's or a compose setup's scripts named, a skipped step in the muted color with **Skipped** where its time would be and the running part open; it follows the log while the job runs, with **Copy log** at the start of its footer and, for a failed job that can start again, **Retry** as its primary button, beside **Edit and retry** for a build, and **Cancel job** in the outline style while a cancel reaches the job; the image drawer explains the image's size with three figures and its history steps, grouped by the image they came from, each with a thin bar against the largest step: the image color for the image's own steps, grey for inherited ones, with the group heading saying which is which.
- **Container cards** show the desktop preview, name, state, desktop, resources and actions. Preserve the full screenshot instead of cropping away parts of the desktop. Hover can strengthen the border but should not move the card.
- **Image rows** use the image icon in its concept color, a cloud for catalog images, and an indented tree for parent relationships; the source and a status badge sit beside the name. A saved image's own icon replaces the line one at 36 px, and selecting either opens the icon dialog. Image selects show the icon too, a cloud for a catalog image. The size column shows Docker's figure and, for an image that shares layers, the part that is its own; a downloaded image's name opens the image drawer.
- **Template cards** show the template's icon at 56 px, its name, tags and description, where a tag the select offers is a button that filters by it and reads as pressed while it does, an **Installed** badge once an image was built from it, and **Install**, which opens **Build image** filled in so the script is read before anything runs. A template that needs a newer release shows that instead of **Install**, and list entries that cannot be offered are listed below the cards with the reason. **Merge and install** turns the cards into choices: a checkbox beside the name, the whole card selectable, a primary ring and a **Step N** badge on each chosen card instead of **Install**, and **Cancel** and **Merge** in the heading, the second enabled from two choices. A list with environments shows two sections, each under a small heading with its icon, **Applications** and then **Environments** with a line saying an environment is merged with an application; search, tags and choosing for a merge span both, and a section the filter leaves empty is left out. A list of one kind has no section headings.
- **Steps and parts** of a compose or build merged from templates are numbered in the order they run, each joined to the next by a line and open: the build form's parts with icon buttons that move one earlier or later or leave it out, the compose editor's steps with **Remove**, and the create form's values by step, each with its value count, **Runs as root** where it does, the saved image it came from and **Ready** in green or a red count of the required values it lacks, every step open so no required value is hidden. A compose or build of one step looks as it did before steps.
- **Dialogs** open one at a time through `core/web/src/lib/dialogs.ts`; the script editor opens over the dialog that holds the script, as the question asked before something is lost does. **New container**, a container's **Settings** and **Build image** are wider forms of one fixed height in sections (`form-sections.tsx`): a menu on the left without borders, each section an icon and its name on one line under small group headings such as **Container** and **Options**, or **Image** and **Build** for a build, the chosen one on the muted tone, and a red count beside **Image setup** while required values are missing; the section's fields beside it scroll on their own, and a line of what is chosen sits beside the buttons. Below 640 px the menu is a strip above the fields. The title, the menu's icons and the fields share one left edge, 24 px in. Every section stays mounted, so a field the browser refuses brings its section into view. Choices of a few values, such as the resolution, the proxy and the lifecycle's lengths, are rows of outline buttons, and features are switches on muted tiles. Explain consequences beside the relevant control: whether a file will be replaced, what a snapshot excludes, why a script runs as root, or which setting restarts the desktop. Destructive confirmations use an alert dialog with a destructive button. A form with changes (`useDraft`) asks **Discard your changes?** before Escape, a click outside, its close button, **Cancel** or a toast's button closes it, so a stray click loses nothing; a dialog that only shows something closes at once. The dialog of a new endpoint token ignores Escape and clicks outside, since it holds the only copy of the token.
- **Toasts** report outcomes the user did not wait for: the end of a job heard on the event stream names the action and what it worked on, and a failure adds the first line of its error; each offers **View log**. A job whose log is open, or one the page that started it shows anyway, such as a script or a download, gets none. An agent's request for a person is a warning toast with **Open desktop**, except on that desktop's own view, where its strip shows it.
- **Render errors** never blank the page: a page that fails to render shows what failed under the header with **Try again** and **Reload**, and the sidebar stays, while a chart or the desktop view that fails shows the same in its own place. A tab left open across a dashboard update reloads by itself once when it needs a chunk the new manager no longer serves, and says **Thoric was updated** if that reload fails too.
- **Backup and restore** on the **Management** page is one card below the release card, with what a backup carries beside choosing a backup to restore, one above the other on a narrow page. Ticking the secret values shows a destructive alert next to the choice. A chosen backup opens an alert dialog that names what the restore creates, what it lacks and, in a destructive alert that disables **Restore**, anything already here under the same name; the upload's progress then stays on the card while the dashboard shows other pages.
- **Network group cards** follow the container cards' grid: the group icon in the container color beside the name in the mono face, the network below it, a badge with how many members run, each member with its aliases as outline badges, and **Open** with an icon button that connects more containers. A group's own page shows its members as container cards with their aliases beside their names and a button that disconnects each one. The dialog that connects containers lists them as checkbox rows with their state; a container the manager would refuse is disabled, with the reason under its name.
- **Name lists**, such as a container's groups and aliases, are a `TagInput`: each name is a removable tag inside the field, added with Enter, a comma or a space, or when the field loses focus, and Backspace in the empty field takes the last one off. Names other containers use open in a suggestion list under the field, each with how many containers list it, and **New** marks a typed name nothing uses yet. A name the field refuses stays in it, marked invalid, so it is corrected rather than lost.
- **File uploads** use a dashed drop target and a compact queue with progress and explicit **Uploaded**, **Failed** or **Cancelled** results. Keep completed outcomes visible and make retry available for unfinished files. Do not offer read-only destinations.

## Small screens and accessibility

Below 768 px the sidebar becomes a sheet, drawers open from the bottom and the chart shows the last 7 days. Summary cards and container cards use container queries: one column on a phone, two on a tablet and four or three on a wide page. Tables scroll inside their frame. A narrow viewport must not scroll the page sideways to reach a form field or action.

A dialog stays inside the window: when its content is taller, the body scrolls between the header and the footer so the actions stay in view. Scripts open in their editor, and other pasted text fields grow with their text up to 500 px and then scroll inside.

Keep keyboard focus visible, give icon buttons accessible names and keep the Radix dialog focus handling. Icons are decorative unless they are the only content of a control. Sign-out must remain reachable on narrow screens. Check text contrast in both themes against the actual surface, not just the page background. Animations are cut short when the system asks for reduced motion.

## Files to change

- `core/web/src/index.css` holds the theme variables, fonts and the few global rules.
- `core/web/src/components/` holds the layout pieces adapted from `dashboard-01` (`app-sidebar.tsx`, `site-header.tsx`, `section-cards.tsx`, `chart-area-interactive.tsx`, `data-table.tsx`) and Thoric's own components; `ui/` holds the shadcn components.
- `core/web/src/pages/` holds one file per page, `core/web/src/components/dialogs/` the dialogs and drawers.
- `core/web/public/mark.svg` is the product mark and favicon: the hammer pendant in flat neutral colors, with one band across the head and the gem. Its dark body and light edge read on both themes; keep it that simple so it survives 16 px.
- `components.json` records the shadcn preset (style, base color, icon library), so components added later with the shadcn CLI match the rest.

Verify interface changes in the live browser, in both themes and at a narrow width, before updating screenshots.
